Section 1
Where TrustLayer sits in the market

The AI governance platform market is projected to reach $492M in 2026 and exceed $1B by 2030 (Gartner, 2025). Despite this scale, almost no vendor in the dedicated AI Governance Platform (AIGP) segment publishes pricing — Credo AI, Holistic AI, Saidot, Modulos, and Trustible all sell by custom enterprise quote. This creates both a challenge and an opportunity for TrustLayer.

The opportunity: TrustLayer has a genuine structural advantage — multi-framework evidence reuse across EU AI Act, ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF, GDPR, and three UAE jurisdictions (mainland, DIFC, ADGM) simultaneously — that no competitor addresses as a native capability. The challenge: the market's incumbent ACV floor is around $50,000/year for a mid-market deployment. TrustLayer's current website pricing ($1,800/month Foundation = $21,600 ACV) is materially below market and risks anchoring perception downward before a single enterprise sales conversation.

Pricing Risk — Immediate Action Required The current published Foundation tier at $1,800/month ($21,600 ACV) sits well below the $50,000 minimum mid-market floor established by OneTrust, Holistic AI, and Credo AI. At this price point, enterprise buyers may question the platform's maturity before any demo. Repricing is a prerequisite to credible enterprise GTM — this document recommends revised ranges before any conference appearance.

Section 3
Four pricing strategies evaluated

Before selecting a model, four structurally distinct approaches were assessed. Each has genuine merit depending on TrustLayer's GTM priority — but they differ significantly in sales complexity, revenue predictability, and expansion ceiling.

Strategy A
Tier-Based Platform SaaS

Three named tiers — Foundation, Professional, Governed Enterprise — differentiated by AI asset caps, user seats, framework count, and advisory access. Fixed annual price per tier, negotiated at renewal.

How it works
Price is anchored to tier selection, not usage metrics. Customers choose a tier based on how many AI systems they need to govern and how many regulatory frameworks they face. The tier determines feature access, support level, and user limits. Most enterprise SaaS platforms use this model (Salesforce, ServiceNow, HubSpot).
Revenue mechanics
Highly predictable ARR. Expansion revenue comes from tier upgrades (horizontal) and renewal uplift (vertical). Net Revenue Retention target should exceed 120% — meaning existing customer expansion outpaces churn. Upsell trigger: customer exceeds AI asset cap or adds a new jurisdiction.

Strategy B
AI System Volume Pricing

A base platform fee plus a per-AI-system charge. As customers register more AI systems in the asset registry, their contract value grows proportionally. The base fee covers platform access; the variable component scales with portfolio size.

How it works
Base platform access fee + a per-system monthly charge. Volume discounts apply at thresholds (1–10 systems at full rate, 11–50 at a discount, 51+ at enterprise rate). This aligns TrustLayer's revenue directly with customers' AI portfolio growth — as they deploy more AI, they pay more, which feels fair to both sides.
Sample pricing
Platform base: $24,000/year. Per-system: $2,400/year (1–10), $1,800/year (11–50), $1,200/year (51+). A customer governing 25 AI systems would pay: $24,000 + ($24,000 × 10 rate) + ($18,000 × 15 rate) = approximately $66,000 ACV. A 100-system enterprise: ~$168,000 ACV.

Strategy C
Framework Licensing

Customers pay a base platform fee and then license individual regulatory frameworks as add-ons. Core platform includes one framework; each additional framework (EU AI Act, ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF, GDPR, UAE DIFC, UAE PDPL, ADGM) is licensed separately.

How it works
Base SaaS platform covers the nine-module architecture, asset registry, risk engine, and audit infrastructure with one framework active. Each additional framework pack is priced as an annual license add-on, with bundle discounts for purchasing multiple. UAE jurisdiction overlays (DIFC, ADGM, mainland PDPL) are treated as separate framework packs.

Strategy D
Outcome-Based / Value-Share Pricing

A small base retainer plus a fee tied to measurable compliance outcomes — audit pass rates, time-to-audit-readiness, evidence package generation milestones, or regulatory deadline clearance. Risk and reward are shared.

How it works
Base platform access fee at a reduced rate, with a success fee triggered on outcome milestones: ISO 42001 certification achieved, EU AI Act conformity assessment passed, FRIA completed on schedule, or mean time to audit readiness falls below 5 business days. Success fee is typically 10–20% of a pre-agreed "value envelope" calculated as avoided regulatory-penalty or audit-preparation cost savings.
When it works
Best for initial enterprise reference customers where TrustLayer is willing to share risk in exchange for a case study, a logo, and a proof point. Not recommended as the primary pricing model at this stage — outcome attribution is difficult to audit, and it creates internal complexity. However, it is a powerful tool in competitive displaccment situations where a prospect hesitates on budget.
Verdict on Strategy D Outcome-based pricing is a sales acceleration tactic rather than a structural model for TrustLayer at this stage. Recommend holding it as a commercial lever for strategic reference customers — particularly first UAE enterprise logos — rather than leading with it in the standard book of business. It adds complexity to ARR forecasting and should be reserved for exceptional circumstances.
Section 4 — Recommendation
Recommended pricing architecture

The recommended model: Strategy A + C hybrid

Lead with three named tiers (Strategy A) for simplicity in sales conversations and fast quoting. Layer on framework pack licensing (Strategy C) as the primary expansion motion — particularly for UAE jurisdiction overlays. Use volume-based scaling (Strategy B) as an implicit mechanism within the Professional and Enterprise tiers via asset cap thresholds, without exposing it as a line-item complexity to the buyer.

This gives TrustLayer a clean answer to "what does it cost?" (Strategy A), a clear expansion story tied to new regulations (Strategy C), and a natural upgrade path without requiring a formal upsell motion (Strategy B thresholds built into tier gates).

UAE/GCC market is handled as a distinct pricing track — not a module code on a tier card, but a named "UAE Compliance Bundle" add-on available on Professional and above, priced to reflect the unique three-jurisdiction complexity that no competitor can match.

Discounting principle Never discount the platform tier price in isolation — attach every discount to a structural commitment (multi-year, upfront, reference, defined expansion milestone). A 20% discount given freely devalues the product; a 20% discount exchanged for a 3-year commitment and a named case study is a deal worth making. The goal is to protect the ACV floor — $42,000 Foundation, $84,000 Professional — even when giving meaningful concessions on structure.
Pricing pages: what to publish vs. what to quote

Following the market norm, TrustLayer should not publish a detailed pricing table on the website — the competitive intelligence risk outweighs the benefit of self-serve conversion at the enterprise ACV range. What to publish: tier names, feature differentiation, and a "starting from" indication at the Foundation level ($42,000/year or equivalent AED). What to gate: specific Professional and Enterprise ACVs, add-on pricing, and discount availability. The current website Pricing page should be revised to match this posture.
