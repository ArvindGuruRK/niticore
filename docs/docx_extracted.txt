1. The strategic positioning
I would not lead with:
“AI Governance Platform”
That's becoming commoditized.
Instead:
TrustLayer = AI Governance Capability Platform
The distinction is important.
Credo, Saidot and Holistic predominantly lead with the technology. Their services then support adoption. Credo, for example, explicitly combines its platform with advisory, training and implementation. Saidot similarly combines its platform with framework design, implementation, professional services and training.
TrustLayer can make the capability journey the central story.
The TrustLayer model
ASSESS → ENABLE → OPERATIONALIZE → ASSURE
That's much more ownable.

I would position TrustLayer as three layers, rather than four unrelated offerings:
TrustLayer Capability Stack
01 — UNDERSTAND
Assess
Know your AI.Know your risks.Know your readiness.
02 — ENABLE
People + Advisory
Build the knowledge, policies, processes and operating model.
03 — OPERATIONALIZE
TrustLayer Platform
Turn governance into an ongoing organizational capability.
So the website story becomes:
Assess → Learn → Design → Operationalize → Assure
And the commercial model naturally becomes:
Assessment → Masterclass → Consulting → Platform
That is a much more compelling customer journey than presenting four disconnected services.

Of all the directions, I would make this the lead:
AI is moving fast.
Governance needs to move faster.
TrustLayer helps organizations build the capability to govern AI — combining expert advisory, practical training and an intelligent governance platform.
Assess. Enable. Operationalize. Assure.

TrustLayer can take that one step further and make “building AI governance capability” the product story itself.
